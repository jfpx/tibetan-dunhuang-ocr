# Research Report: bSam gtan mig sgron (禅定目炬)
# *A Lamp for the Eyes of Contemplation*

**Author**: gNubs chen Sangs rgyas ye shes (Nubchen Sangye Yeshe), early 10th century  
**Research date**: 2026-09-14  
**Purpose**: Locate digital sources and scholarly translations for Chan↔Dzogchen cross-tradition comparison

---

## 1. Overview of the Text

The *bSam gtan mig sgron* ("Lamp for the Eyes of Contemplation") is a 10th-century Tibetan doxographical treatise by Nubchen Sangye Yeshe. It systematically compares four meditation systems:

1. **Chapter 1**: The Gradual System (*rim gyis 'jug pa*) — Indian Buddhist gradualism (Kamalaśīla's tradition)
2. **Chapter 2**: The Simultaneous System (*cig car 'jug pa*) — Chinese Chan/禅 (Heshang Moheyan's tradition)
3. **Chapter 3**: Mahāyoga — Tantric meditation system
4. **Chapter 4**: Atiyoga/Dzogchen (*rdzogs chen*) — The Great Perfection

**Why this text is uniquely valuable**: It is the only surviving systematic Tibetan comparison that treats Chinese Chan as a legitimate (though subordinate) path, ranking it *above* Indian gradualism but *below* Vajrayāna and Dzogchen. Written before the post-debate anti-Chan orthodoxy hardened, it preserves a remarkably balanced assessment.

- Scanned page images (BDRC)
- Excerpts quoted in scholarly articles
- The published critical edition (Esler 2022, behind paywall)

## 4. Sections Most Relevant for Chan↔Dzogchen Comparison

### 4.1 Chapter 2: The Simultaneous System (cig car 'jug pa)

This is the **highest-priority chapter**. It presents:
- Heshang Moheyan's meditation instructions (Chinese Chan method)
- The five graduated techniques for different faculty levels (from Pelliot tibétain 117 and bSam gtan mig sgron p.165):
  1. Direct awareness of arising deluded thoughts
  2. Examination of that awareness
  3. Prevention of thought-arising
  4. Perception that thoughts have no intrinsic nature (emptiness)
  5. Awareness without analysis — thoughts freed the instant they arise
- The "sharpest faculties" approach: letting thoughts self-liberate without suppression
- Key Tibetan passage (from p.165): *de bas na 'du shes dgag par yang mi bya / 'byung bzhin ci la yang mi bcos par gyi na ye gtang ji bzhin du bzhag dang rang zhi ste rjes su mi 'brang ngo //* ("Therefore you should not suppress concepts. Whenever they arise, if you do not fabricate anything but instead let them go, then they will stay as they are and come to rest by themselves.")

### 4.2 Chapter 4: Atiyoga/Dzogchen

- The Great Perfection meditation system
- Key distinction: *sems* (mind) vs *rig pa* (awareness) — the criterion Nubchen uses to differentiate Dzogchen from Chan
- Nubchen's ranking: Chan is valid but subordinate to Dzogchen

### 4.3 Cross-Chapter Comparison Points

- The self-liberation of thoughts: shared theme between Chan and Dzogchen, but with different theoretical frameworks
- Non-conceptuality (*mi rtog pa*): both traditions value it, but Dzogchen adds the *rig pa*/*sems* distinction
- Nubchen's explicit statement that he writes about Chan *because* of its similarities with Dzogchen, which could cause confusion

## 6. CBETA and Chinese Digital Resources

- **CBETA** (Chinese Buddhist Electronic Text Association, `https://cbeta.org`): Contains Chinese Buddhist canonical texts but **not** direct translations of *bSam gtan mig sgron* (it is a Tibetan-language text, never translated into Chinese historically)
- **Relevant Chinese texts on CBETA**: Search for texts related to Moheyan (摩诃衍), the Samyé debate (桑耶辩论/拉萨法诤), and Chan meditation manuals that may parallel content in the *bSam gtan mig sgron*
- **SAT Daizōkyō Database** (`https://21dzk.l.u-tokyo.ac.jp/SAT/`): Japanese/Chinese canonical search
- The Chinese dimension of this text is primarily *about* Chinese Chan as described in Tibetan — the original Chinese Chan texts that Nubchen drew upon are partially preserved in Dunhuang Chinese manuscripts (Pelliot chinois collection)
