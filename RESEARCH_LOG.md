## 1. IOL Tib J 689 — bSam gtan mig sgron (Lamp for the Eye in Meditation/Contemplation)

### Author
gNubs chen Sangs rgyas ye shes (Nubchen Sangye Yeshe), 9th century. One of the 25 principal disciples of Padmasambhava. Preserved tantric lineages through the Tibetan "dark period" (842–978 CE).

### Significance
The single most important Dunhuang Tibetan meditation text. Synthesizes four contemplative systems:
1. **Gradual Vehicle** (Bodhisattvayana) — Indian scholastic meditation
2. **Sudden Mahayana** (Chan/Zen) — Chinese instantaneous approach
3. **Mahāyoga** — Tantric deity yoga
4. **Atiyoga/Dzogchen** — Great Perfection

Discovered at Dunhuang by Paul Pelliot in 1908. Its entombment since the 11th–12th century guarantees textual authenticity.

### IDP Access
- **IDP URL**: `https://idp.bl.uk/database/oo_loader.a4d?pm=IOL%20Tib%20J%20689`
- **Status**: IDP website returned 403 errors on all attempts (site may be blocking automated requests or temporarily down). Manual browser access recommended.
- The manuscript is held at the **British Library** (IOL = India Office Library collection).

**Dylan Esler's 2022 OUP translation is the complete scholarly edition.** This changes the OCR calculus dramatically — a full critical edition with English translation already exists.

### Description
~60 folios of detailed Mahāyoga tantric practice instructions.

### IDP Access

- **Status**: IDP returned 403 on all search attempts. Held at **Bibliothèque nationale de France** (Pelliot collection).

### Existing Scholarship
- Jacob Dalton has worked extensively on Nubchen's tantric works, particularly the *Armor Against Darkness* (mun pa'i go cha) which comments on related Anuyoga material.

## 3. Pelliot tibétain 699 (Pt 699) — Cig car 'jug pa'i sgom don (Sudden Meditation)

### Description
~15 folios on "sudden entry" meditation practice. Related to the Chan/Zen-influenced sudden approach discussed in the bSam gtan mig sgron.

### IDP Access
- **IDP URL**: `https://idp.bl.uk/database/oo_loader.a4d?pm=Pelliot%20tib%C3%A9tain%20699`

## 5. Recommended Approach

### For IOL Tib J 689 (bSam gtan mig sgron)
**DO NOT OCR the raw manuscript.** Instead:
1. **Obtain Dylan Esler's 2022 OUP critical edition** — this contains the established Tibetan text and full English translation
2. Use Karmay's edition for cross-reference
3. Download the Khenchen Palden Sherab commentary PDF (already confirmed accessible)
4. Manuscript images from IDP remain useful for paleographic study but OCR is redundant

### For Pt 116 and Pt 699
1. **Access IDP via browser** (automated access blocked) to download manuscript images
2. **Also check Gallica/BnF** for Pelliot tibétain collection digitizations
3. **Check TBRC/BDRC** (Buddhist Digital Resource Center, `library.bdrc.io`) for any existing digital texts
4. If no transcriptions exist, these are genuine OCR candidates — but Tibetan manuscript OCR (especially dbu med script) remains extremely challenging
5. Consider whether manual transcription by a Tibetologist would be more reliable than OCR

### Technical Notes on Tibetan OCR
- Dunhuang manuscripts are typically in **dbu med** (headless) cursive script, much harder to OCR than modern dbu can (headed) print
- Google's Tesseract has limited Tibetan support (mainly for modern print)
- Specialized tools: **Namsel** (developed for Tibetan OCR) or **Transkribus** (HTR platform with trainable models) are better options
- For these manuscripts, **Handwritten Text Recognition (HTR)** rather than traditional OCR is the appropriate technology
